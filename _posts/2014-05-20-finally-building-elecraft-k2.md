![Picture of an Elecraft K2](/assets/images/2014/05/k2100.JPG)

Back in 2003, yes 2003, I splashed out and bought an [Elecraft
K2](https://elecraft.com/collections/full-kits-1/products/k2-the-classic-hf-transceiver-full-kit "Elecraft K2").
To my mind at the time, it was the best radio there was at a price point
I was prepared to pay. (*Note: Even now, it\'s still right up there with
the best*). Also, it came as a kit; which attracted me. I did a lot of
circuit construction in my early days as an engineer and I have kept my
eye in over the years.

I started to build it, and got as far as completing the front panel and
control boards. Then life intervened (*big time!*) and the K2 ended up
in a box awaiting further work.

Actually, I was able to wind the toroids during this time. In 2005 I spent 
six months *commuting* monthly between
London and Cape Town on a customer project. I was lucky to be flying BA first class
and did a deal with the BA operations staff (BA was also a customer of ours) to let me
spend some of the time on the overnight flight winding toroids.
They kept my components air-side and they were waiting for me when I boarded.
Over the months, the cabin crew got used to me playing with toroids and it became quite a talking point.

Well the time has finally arrived to make progress again. It\'s only been 11 years after all!

The kit is on the bench and I\'ve started to build the RF board. I try
and spend an hour a day on it. I usually work from home, so I get to
spend the hour between stopping work at six and supper time at seven to
do some construction. *It\'s better than spending it stuck in a traffic
jam*. If I\'m lucky, I get another hour in after supper.
I started in the middle of May and progress is good.

The best investment has undoubtedly been the [Panavise
333](https://panavise.com/index.html?pageID=1&page=full&--eqskudatarq=26 "Panavise")
circuit board holder. It was expensive, but definitely worth it.
